## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This document records the **visual design decisions** of the Zangbundel app: which screens look alike, what the welcome screen shows, how the background image is used and sized, how the two themes behave, and the rules for text, contrast and system bars. It is the document the FRD's section 19 lists as `design/ui-flows.md` ("screen flows for leader and singer, day/night theme guidelines"), under its final name; screen-by-screen flows will be added to it in a later version.

Every decision is numbered **D-nn** so that later documents, code comments and reviews can refer to it. A decision that turns out wrong is replaced by a new one and the old one is marked *Superseded*, as the FRD does with requirements.

### 1.2 Scope

In scope: the welcome screen, the home screen and the two top-level modes, the configuration screens (first-use introduction, permission explanations, privacy screen, settings, library management, About), the gathering screens (lyric view and everything shown at the fire), the background image, themes and colours, typography, contrast, touch targets, system bars.

Out of scope: the wording of each screen's texts, which lives in the locale files (section 8 gives the rules they follow), the architecture (which UI toolkit), and the app icon, which is decided in `design/icon/`.

### 1.3 Conventions

MUST / SHOULD / MAY as in RFC 2119. Requirement IDs in brackets point to the FRD. Dutch UI terms in parentheses. Sizes in **dp** (density-independent pixels) for layout and **sp** for text, as on Android; an iOS implementation maps them to points one to one.

## 2. Two screen families

The app has two kinds of screens that look deliberately different, so a user can tell at a glance whether they are *setting up* or *singing*.

| Family | Screens | Background | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Configuration** (*instellen*) | Welcome screen, first-use introduction [FR-GEN-06], permission explanations [FR-GEN-05], privacy screen [FR-GEN-08], home screen (join or start, section 4), settings, library management and import reports, About [FR-GEN-07] | One shared background image under a dark scrim (section 5), white text | Before and between gatherings, mostly with light around |
| **Gathering** (*samenzingen*) | Lyric view, setlist, song requests, gathering list and join dialogs, leader controls, delegate screens, "what changed" after an update | No image. Night theme: white text on pure black. Day theme: black text on pure white [FR-DISP-01] | At the fire, often in the dark |

**D-48 — The phone is not the attraction.** *(Added in v0.5; a design objective that ranks above every later decision in this document.)* Singers are children at a campfire, and the app's job is to put the words in front of them and then get out of the way. Every feature on a singer screen is judged by one question: does it give a child a reason to look at the phone instead of singing? Features that invite fiddling, collecting, competing or exploring are held back or left out, however small: no counters, scores, badges or streaks; no animations beyond the one-second cue for a new song; no sounds; no social feedback (a singer never sees how many others liked a song); no customisation beyond what reading in the dark needs; and nothing to browse or zoom that the leader did not send. When a requirement of the FRD can be met in a plainer or a richer way, the plainer way wins; a richer way needs a reason written next to it. The table below lists what this objective has already held back. *Why:* the owner's objective for the singer side, 2026-10-19: minimise the incentives for a child to start playing with the app and the phone, and keep the attention on the singing. A feature that is fun to build is the easiest way to lose that.

| Held back by D-48 | Instead |
|---|---|
| "+1" on other singers' requests (FR-REQ-07, MAY) | Not in phase 1; the request tab shows only the singer's own requests (D-38). |
| Favourites star in singer mode (FR-LIB-32, SHOULD) | The star exists only in the library outside a gathering; the *Liedboek* tab shows no star while joined (D-39, D-40). |
| Variant chip on the song title (D-41) | The variant switch lives in the song-info sheet (D-45), one layer down. |
| Like counts, "most liked" lists, request leaderboards | Never shown to singers; the leader sees the counts (D-32). |
| Magnifier mode (Q13) | Zooming and panning is play; the investigation must show a readability gain that the text-size steps cannot give, or the idea is dropped. |
| Themes, colours, fonts to choose from | Day and night only (D-15); the system font (D-18). |
| Reading ahead in the setlist | Only songs the leader sent (D-47). |

**D-01 — Two visual families.** Configuration screens share one background image; gathering screens never show an image. The border between the families is the moment a gathering starts or is joined, and the moment it ends. *Why:* at the fire the display must not dazzle [FR-DISP-03], must read at arm's length [FR-DISP-05] and must spend as little battery as possible [NFR-PERF-02]; a photograph works against all three. Before the gathering, the image gives the app a face and makes the setup screens recognisable.

**D-02 — Night is the campfire default.** When a gathering starts or is joined, the gathering screens open in the night theme unless the user set the theme to day or to "follow the device" [FR-DISP-02]. The one-action theme switch stays in the lyric view [FR-DISP-01]. *Why:* a campfire is dark; a white screen is the most common complaint about phone songbooks.

**D-03 — The theme setting also reaches configuration screens, but only as a scrim level.** In the day theme the background image is shown under a light scrim; in the night theme the configuration screens drop the image entirely and use the gathering family's black background. *Why:* FR-DISP-03 forbids large bright areas in the night theme, and a singer who opens Settings at the fire must not be dazzled. A photo "dimmed to 15 %" still shows highlights; dropping it is simpler, consistent, and cheaper on an OLED screen.

## 3. Welcome screen (*welkomstscherm*)

**D-04 — The welcome screen shows the app name, the app version and the icon artwork, nothing else.** Content, top to bottom, centred: the icon artwork from `design/icon/zangbundel-icon.svg` (the seven singers, the fire and the open songbook with the Bluetooth sign, without the launcher's rounded background) at 96 dp; the name **Zangbundel** in the display size; the version line `versie 1.0.0` / `version 1.0.0` in the body size, using `versionName` from the build (`docs/RUNBOOK.md` Decisions). No tagline, no tips, no progress bar, no "powered by".

**D-05 — Short and skippable.** The welcome screen is shown for at most **1.5 seconds** after the app is ready, or until the user taps it, whichever is first. It is never shown when the app is opened to resume a gathering from its resume data or from the join link, and never a second time while the app stays in memory. *Why:* a splash that delays the user is a cost; this one only makes the start recognisable and carries the version for support questions ("which version do you have?" is answered by restarting the app).

**D-06 — The system splash comes first.** Android 12 and later show a system splash with the launcher icon before any app content. The welcome screen is designed to follow it without a jump: the system splash background is the icon's night blue (`#0B2545`), and the welcome screen opens with the icon artwork at the same position, then fades in the background image, name and version over 300 ms. Users with reduced motion enabled get the final frame at once. *Why:* two unrelated splashes in a row look broken.

**D-07 — First use continues from the welcome screen.** On the first start, the welcome screen is followed by the first-use introduction [FR-GEN-06], which links to the privacy screen before any permission is requested [FR-GEN-08]. On later starts it is followed by the home screen (section 4). The introduction and the privacy screen are configuration screens and use the same background.

## 4. Home screen and the two modes

### 4.1 Leader and singer

**D-19 — At the top level the app is in one of two modes, leader (*kampvuurleider*) or singer (*zanger*), or in neither.** Every installation can take either role [FR-GEN-01]; a device is never in both at once [FR-GEN-02]. The state the app shows at all times [FR-GEN-04] is one of: *idle* (home screen, no gathering), *searching* (home screen, scanning), *leading*, *joined*, *reconnecting* [FR-SING-04], *importing a library*. The rest of this document, and every screen flow added to it later, is written per mode: a gathering screen exists in a **leader variant** and a **singer variant**. The two variants share the lyric view [FR-LEAD-04], the setlist and the theme, text-size and brightness controls (D-17); they differ in the controls that act on the gathering. The leader variant adds push, next, skip, requests and the singer count [FR-LEAD-03, FR-GATH-12]; the singer variant adds leave [FR-GATH-07] and the request form. *Why:* one mental model for users and one navigation tree per role for implementers; a screen that is "almost the same" in both roles is one screen with role-dependent controls, never two screens.

**D-20 — The mode is always visible.** Every gathering screen carries a one-line header strip in the secondary text colour: in leader mode the gathering name and the number of singers (`Kampvuur 18 okt · 12 zangers`), in singer mode the gathering name and the connection state (`Kampvuur 18 okt · verbonden` / `· opnieuw verbinden…` / `· beëindigd`) [FR-SING-04, FR-GATH-09, FR-GATH-10]. In the lyric view this strip is the header of FR-DISP-11. The strip is the only place the mode is spelled out; the role is otherwise shown by which controls exist.

### 4.2 Home screen (*startscherm*): join or start

The home screen follows the welcome screen (D-05) and is the root of the configuration family: it is where the user leaves to become a leader or a singer, and where they return when a gathering ends.

**D-21 — Two actions, and nothing competes with them.** The home screen offers exactly two primary actions: **join a gathering** (*meezingen*) and **start a gathering** (*kampvuur starten*). The songbook (*Liedboek*, D-52), memories (D-49), settings and About are reachable from a small row of icons at the bottom and never take space from the two actions; the libraries are managed from the songbook (D-50) *(amended in v0.16)*. *Why:* FR-GEN-01 says the user chooses the role when starting or joining; this screen is that choice and nothing else.

**D-22 — Scanning starts on the home screen, and what it finds is the screen.** On entering the home screen the app scans for gatherings [FR-GATH-03] and keeps scanning while the screen is shown. The layout depends on the result:

| State | Join area (top, large) | Start area (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| **Gatherings in range** | One card per gathering, the strongest signal first: name, signal strength or distance indication [FR-GATH-04], a lock icon when a join code is needed [FR-GATH-05]. Cards are tall (72 dp minimum) and fill the width; tapping one joins it. The header reads *Kampvuren in de buurt*. | A single small text button, *Zelf een kampvuur starten*, in the secondary text colour. Starting is refused while a gathering is listed, see D-23. |
| **None found yet** (first 5 seconds and later) | A quiet scanning indicator, *Zoeken naar kampvuren…*, and one line of help: scan the leader's QR code with the camera app to join [FR-GATH-23]. No empty list, no error. | The start button is large and primary (fire orange), since starting is the likely intention. |
| **Bluetooth off or permission missing** | A card that explains in plain language why Bluetooth (and, on Android 11 and lower, location) is needed and offers the system dialog [FR-GEN-05]; nothing is requested before the user taps. | Start button present but disabled with the same explanation. |
| **Resume available** [FR-GATH-13] | A card above the list: *Kampvuur 18 okt hervatten?* with resume and discard. | As above. |

QR-only gatherings [FR-GATH-21] are not in the list by design; they are joined through the QR code only, and a phone that opens the join link skips the home screen and goes straight to joining. *Why:* a singer at a fire should need one tap; a leader setting up usually has no gathering around and gets the start button where the thumb is. The two states are the same screen with the proportions reversed, not two screens.

**D-23 — The one-gathering rule is enforced on this screen.** FR-GATH-16 requires listening for at least 5 seconds before a gathering may start. Because the home screen is already scanning, the start action simply reads the current list: if the screen has been open for less than 5 seconds the start button shows the remaining time (*even luisteren… 3 s*); if a gathering is listed, starting is refused with the gathering's name and a join button, exactly as the requirement says. There is no separate "checking" dialog.

**D-24 — A device that cannot lead says so, once.** When the platform reports no BLE peripheral (advertising) support (FRD constraint 1), the start area shows *Dit toestel kan meezingen, maar geen kampvuur starten* instead of the button. The join area is unchanged.

**D-82 — Starting needs songs.** *(Added in v0.19 at the owner's request; not built yet.)* While no library with songs is installed (the songbook of D-52 is empty), *Kampvuur starten* is shown greyed out. A tap on it does not start anything; it answers with one line, *Laad eerst liederen: pas dan kun je een kampvuur starten*, and a button to the songbook (D-52), where the libraries are added (D-50). Joining is unaffected: a singer needs no songs. *Why:* the owner's instruction; a campfire without songs has nothing to push, and the leader learns at the start, not at the fire, what is missing.

**D-25 — Start leads into leader mode through one screen.** Tapping start opens the gathering settings screen (a configuration screen): the proposed neutral name [FR-GATH-01], the optional join code and the QR-only switch [FR-GATH-05, FR-GATH-21], and the setlist to begin with [FR-LEAD-02]. Its primary button *Starten* switches the app into leader mode and the gathering family: the lyric view (empty until the first song is pushed) with the leader controls and the join QR code one tap away [FR-DIST-02].

**D-26 — Join leads into singer mode through at most one screen.** Tapping a gathering card joins it directly when it is open; when it needs a join code, a dialog asks for the code first. The first join ever also asks for the optional display name [FR-SING-01] on the same dialog, and never again unless the user changes it in settings. After joining, the app switches into singer mode and the gathering family: the current song is shown at once [FR-GATH-06].

## 5. Background image (*achtergrondafbeelding*)

### 5.1 One image, one asset

**D-08 — One image for every configuration screen.** The welcome screen, the first-use introduction, the permission explanations, the privacy screen, the home screen, settings, library management and About all draw the same image, placed identically (centre-cropped to the full screen, section 5.3). The image carries no information: every screen reads the same with the image replaced by a flat colour. *Why:* one asset keeps the app small, which matters for offline sharing of the package [FR-DIST-05], and a single placement makes the screens feel like one app.

### 5.2 Content of the image

**D-09 — Subject, palette and people.** The image shows a campfire scene at dusk or night, in the palette of the app icon: night blue (`#0B2545` to `#13315C`) and fire orange (`#FF8A3D`, `#FFB36B`), with the dark surround taking most of the area and the fire as the light source. It contains **no recognisable faces**: people may appear seen from behind or as silhouettes, as singers at a fire do, but never with a face that can be matched to a person; no group names, badges, flags or logos, and no text. Consent is needed only for a person who can be recognised in the image; the owner judges that and records the judgement in `design/gui/README.md` before the image is published *(amended in v0.9; v0.3 asked for parental consent whenever children appear)*. It is a photograph the project holds the rights to, or an illustration made for the project; the licence and credits are recorded in `design/gui/README.md`. *Why:* privacy by design (FRD 2.6) rules out identifiable minors, and faces are what makes a person identifiable; singers seen from behind are what the app is about; a neutral scene fits every scouting group; text in an image cannot be localised [FR-LANG-01]; the palette ties the screens to the icon. *(Amended in v0.3; v0.1 excluded people altogether.)*

**D-10 — The image is dark by itself.** Average luminance of the image is below 35 % and the top third of the frame, where titles sit, is the darkest part. *Why:* the scrim (D-13) then only has to guarantee contrast, not create it, and the image survives being dropped in the night theme without the screens changing character.

### 5.3 Size and format

The question to answer is: **what size should the background image be?** Phones in use range from 16:9 (older, 1080 × 1920) through 19.5:9 (1080 × 2340) and 20:9 (1080 × 2400, 1440 × 3200) to 22:9; tablets are 16:10 and 4:3. The image is always *centre-cropped* to fill the screen (Android `ContentScale.Crop` / `centerCrop`, iOS `scaledToFill`), so the one asset only has to be tall enough that no common phone scales it up.

**D-11 — The shipped image is 1440 × 3200 pixels, portrait, 9:20.** That is the largest common phone resolution (QHD+ at 20:9). On a 1080 × 2400 phone it is scaled down by 0.75; on a 1080 × 1920 phone the sides are kept and 320 rows top and bottom fall off; on a 1440 × 3200 phone it is shown 1:1. No phone scales it up. It is shipped **once, without density variants**: on Android in `res/drawable-nodpi/` (so the platform does not rescale it per density; the view crops it), on iOS as a single-scale image in the asset catalog. *Why:* a photograph behind a scrim does not benefit from per-density copies, and one file is the smallest download.

**D-12 — Format and weight.** The shipped file is lossy **WebP** at quality 80 (Android 8.0, the minimum SDK, decodes it natively; iOS 14 and later as well), target **under 300 KB**, hard limit 500 KB. The master is kept outside the app at its native resolution, in `design/gui/` with its licence note (`design/gui/README.md`): the source photograph, the Paint.NET project that holds the crop and edit, the 1440 × 3200 PNG master, and the WebP export derived from it by the documented procedure so it can be re-exported. If the image is an illustration rather than a photograph, it MAY be shipped as a vector (Android `VectorDrawable`, iOS PDF) instead and then has no pixel size; the composition rules below still apply.

**Safe area and composition.** Because the frame is cropped on wide, short and landscape screens, the subject is composed within guard zones:

| Zone (of the 1440 × 3200 frame) | Rule |
|---|---|
| Centre 1440 × 2560 (rows 320–2880) | Everything that matters is here: the fire, the horizon. This is what a 16:9 phone shows. |
| Top 400 rows and bottom 520 rows | Only sky and ground: may be cut on short screens; on tall screens the status bar and the bottom navigation or buttons cover them. |
| Centre band 1440 × 650 (rows 1275–1925) | What a landscape phone shows (section 5.4). The fire or the horizon must be visible here. |
| Left and right 180 columns | May be cut on tablets in portrait and in some landscape crops. |

**Tablets.** A 1600 × 2560 (10-inch, 16:10) tablet in portrait shows the image scaled to width 1600 (factor 1.11, a slight upscale that is invisible behind the scrim). Landscape on tablets, if a tablet layout is ever in scope (FRD Q7), gets its own 2560 × 1600 export from the same master (Q2 below).

### 5.4 Orientation

**D-13 — Configuration screens work in both orientations, with a separate landscape image.** *(Amended in v0.3.)* Landscape gets its own export, **3200 × 1440 pixels (20:9)**, composed from the same source photograph so both orientations show the same scene, shipped like the portrait one (WebP, quality 80, under 300 KB, no metadata) as `backdrop-3200x1440.webp` in `res/drawable-land-nodpi/` on Android and as the landscape trait variant in the iOS asset catalog. Its guard zones mirror the portrait ones: everything that matters within the centre 2560 × 1440 (columns 320–2880), the light source (fire or lit songbook) inside those columns, and the top 180 rows free for the status bar. The landscape composition puts the photograph at the right and leaves the left part black, so titles and buttons sit on black and the picture is never covered. Should the landscape export ever be missing, the portrait image is centre-cropped (a 2400 × 1080 screen then shows the central 1440 × 650 band, scaled by 1.67). *Why:* v0.1 accepted that crop to save one asset, but with the chosen photograph the centre band shows the dark middle of the scene rather than the fire, and a second 60 KB asset is cheaper than a poor landscape welcome screen. Portrait-locking the setup screens stays out of the question: it is hostile to tablets and to a mounted phone.

### 5.5 Scrim and contrast

**D-14 — A scrim guarantees contrast; the image never does.** Over the image lies a vertical gradient from `#0B2545` at 60 % opacity at the top to `#000000` at 75 % opacity at the bottom, plus, behind any block of body text (the privacy screen, an import report), a solid panel of `#0B2545` at 85 %. All text on configuration screens is white (`#FFFFFF`) or off-white (`#CFE6FF` for secondary text); the primary button is fire orange (`#FF8A3D`) with dark text (`#0B2545`). The contrast of white body text over the darkest and the lightest point of the scrimmed image MUST both exceed **4.5 : 1** (WCAG AA) and SHOULD exceed 7 : 1; the check is part of adding or replacing the image. *Why:* FR-DISP-10 demands AA in the lyric view; the configuration screens should not be worse, and a scrim with fixed opacities makes the result independent of the photograph.

## 6. Gathering screens

**D-15 — Pure black and pure white, nothing in between for backgrounds.** Night theme: background `#000000`, lyric text `#FFFFFF`, secondary text `#BFBFBF`, dividers `#2A2A2A`. Day theme: background `#FFFFFF`, lyric text `#000000`, secondary `#444444`, dividers `#DDDDDD`. Dialogs, lists, buttons and the system bars follow the theme [FR-DISP-03]. No image, no gradient, no card shadows. *Why:* FR-DISP-01 defines the themes by these colours; pure black switches OLED pixels off, which NFR-PERF-02 relies on for a four-hour evening.

**D-16 — The current line is marked by weight and a side bar, not by a bright area.** The current line [FR-DISP-09] is bold, and a 4 dp bar in fire orange (`#FF8A3D`) runs along its left edge; the text colour does not change, so the highlight survives colour-blindness and dimmed screens. In the night theme the bar is the only coloured element on the screen. The header and footer of FR-DISP-11 use the secondary text colour at the smallest text size.

**D-17 — The theme switch, text size and brightness are one tap away.** *(Amended in v0.4.)* A tap on the header strip (D-20), or a long press anywhere on the lyrics, shows a translucent toolbar for three seconds with: theme toggle [FR-DISP-01], text size smaller/larger [FR-DISP-05], fit-to-width toggle (D-28), brightness slider [FR-DISP-08], keep-screen-on toggle [FR-DISP-07], follow-the-leader toggle [FR-SING-12], and song info [FR-SING-15]. A single tap on the lyrics themselves is reserved for scrolling: lower half one line down, upper half one line up [FR-SING-11]; v0.1 had the toolbar on a single tap, which conflicted with that requirement. The toolbar is dark in both themes, so it never dazzles.

**QR code at the fire.** The join QR code is the one exception to the night theme: it is shown black on a white panel with the brightness raised [FR-DIST-02]. The panel is as small as the scanning distance allows, and the rest of the screen stays black.

### 6.1 Singer mode: the lyric view (*liedtekst*)

The singer's lyric view is the screen the app exists for. It is one continuous page that holds the whole setlist of the session, not one song at a time, and it gives every pixel it can to the words.

**D-27 — Maximum screen use.** In singer mode the lyric view is edge to edge: no app bar, the status bar hidden (section 7), the system navigation bar in the theme's background colour, and margins of 16 dp left and right. The only fixed element is the header strip of D-20, 24 dp high at the top, with the song title, the position in the setlist, the connection state and, when switched on, the follow-the-leader mark [FR-DISP-11, FR-SING-04]. The three round buttons (D-30) float over the text. Nothing else is on screen. *Why:* legibility from arm's length at the fire [FR-DISP-05] is the first requirement; everything that is not a lyric costs lines.

**D-28 — Lines always wrap; landscape adds fit-to-width.** The FRD makes wrapping a MUST in both orientations: horizontal scrolling must never be needed [FR-DISP-06]. The evaluation for this document: at the default 28 sp a 1080-pixel portrait screen holds about 30 characters per line, so most lyric lines (30 to 50 characters, at most 120 by the song format) wrap once; a 2400-pixel landscape screen holds about 70, so most lines fit unwrapped. Wrapping therefore stays on in both orientations and is rarely visible in landscape. On top of that the view offers **fit-to-width** (*passend maken*): when on, the text size is reduced by at most two steps of D-18 so that the longest line of the song fits on one line; when even that is not enough, the line wraps. Wrapped continuation lines get a hanging indent of 1 em so a wrapped line is never mistaken for a new line of the song [FR-SING-10]. Default: fit-to-width off in portrait, on in landscape (Q10). *Why:* "no wrapping in landscape" as a hard rule would break FR-DISP-06 for long lines and very large text sizes; fit-to-width gives the same result where it is possible and degrades gracefully where it is not.

**D-29 — One page for the whole setlist; previous and next song at the ends.** The lyric view scrolls by swipe for continuous scrolling and by tap for one line (D-17) [FR-SING-11]; the volume keys scroll one line when enabled in settings. The page is not one song: above the current song's title stands the previous song of the setlist, below its last line the next one, each separated by a divider with the song's title and its position (*3/12*). Scrolling past a divider makes that song the one the header strip names. Which songs are on the page is fixed by D-47: only the songs the leader has pushed in this session, in the order pushed; there is no upcoming song and no placeholder. Any manual scroll switches follow-the-leader off [FR-SING-12]; while the singer is away from the current song a chip *Terug naar het huidige lied* sits at the bottom centre, and one tap returns [FR-SING-14]. When the leader pushes a new song the page scrolls to it with the cue of FR-SING-02, wherever the singer was. Jumping to the previous or next verse or chorus [FR-SING-13] is a horizontal swipe within the song. *Why:* at a fire the next song is the natural question; reading ahead or back is part of singing along, and it must never require leaving the view.

**D-30 — Three round buttons, bottom right, stacked.** Over the lyrics, 16 dp from the right and bottom edges, a vertical column of three round buttons of 56 dp with 12 dp between them, top to bottom: **previous song** (chevron up), **songlist** (list icon), **next song** (chevron down). Previous and next move the page to the start of the neighbouring song among the songs received in this session (D-47); next is disabled on the most recently pushed song. They navigate the singer's own view and never control the leader. The buttons are dark translucent in both themes (night: `#1A1A1A` at 80 % with white icons; day: `#E6E6E6` at 80 % with black icons) so they do not dazzle [FR-DISP-03], dim to 40 % opacity after three seconds without touch and return to full on any touch. In landscape the column stays at the bottom right. A left-handed setting mirrors the column to the left (Q12). *Why:* thumb reach on a phone held in one hand, in the dark, with the other hand around a mug; 56 dp exceeds the 48 dp minimum of NFR-ACC-01 on purpose.

**D-31 — The songlist is the session's setlist, current song first.** The middle button opens the songlist (*liedjeslijst*) as a bottom sheet in the theme's colours: the current song pinned at the top with an orange bar, then the songs of this session in the order the leader pushed them, earlier ones with a check mark [FR-SING-03]; nothing the leader has not sent yet (D-47). Tapping a song closes the sheet and jumps the lyric view to the start of that song; tapping the pinned current song is the one-action return of FR-SING-14. Each row carries the like control of D-32. The sheet's last row is *Kampvuur verlaten* (D-34). The singer's own request list [FR-REQ-05] is a second tab of the same sheet, so the sheet is the one place for everything about the session that is not the lyrics.

**D-32 — Likes: a heart on every song, sent to the leader as a request.** A heart (*vind ik leuk*) sits at the right of every songlist row and at the start of every song on the lyric page, next to its title. Tapping it sends a like for that song to the leader; tapping again withdraws it [FR-REQ-04]. One like per singer per song per gathering. On the leader's side a like is a request for that song [FR-REQ-01]: likes for the same song are grouped and counted [FR-LEAD-12], so the request list [FR-LEAD-10] shows the most-liked songs on top and the leader can prioritise. A like for the current song counts as "sing it again later". Likes travel encrypted to the leader like any request [FR-BLE-17]; the leader sees the count, not who liked (Q11). Likes do not count against the pending-request limit of FR-REQ-06, since they name songs the leader already has. *Why:* a one-tap, anonymous signal is the request mechanism children will actually use; typing a title is for songs that are not on the list.

**D-33 — Song info stays one tap away but off the page.** The metadata of the song (author, licence, source, library) [FR-SING-15] opens from the toolbar (D-17) or from a long press on the song's title; it is not printed on the lyric page.

**D-34 — Leaving singer mode is deliberate.** The exit from singer mode is the row *Kampvuur verlaten* at the bottom of the songlist sheet (D-31), and the system back gesture, both followed by the confirmation that FR-GATH-07 requires (*Je ziet de liedteksten dan niet meer.* with *Verlaten* and *Blijven*). It is not one of the three round buttons, so it cannot be hit by accident in the dark. Leaving returns to the home screen (section 4) at once and tells the leader in the background, best effort for up to ten seconds, so the singer count drops (BLE protocol 10.11 as of v0.29, Control op 10; amended in v0.19), ends the singer mode header, and discards the lyrics received over the air [FR-DATA-01]; the gathering memory of FR-DATA-03 is written at that moment. When the leader ends the gathering, the lyric view stays readable [FR-GATH-09] and the header strip becomes a 48 dp **orange bar** with a small warning sign, *<name> is beëindigd · tik om te verlaten*; a tap on it leaves, without a confirmation since there is nothing left to leave *(amended in v0.19 at the owner's request; the bar is the only bright area of the night theme and appears only once the gathering is over, so FR-DISP-03 is kept)*.

**D-47 — A singer sees only what the leader has sent.** The setlist page (D-29), the *Lijst* tab (D-31) and the previous/next buttons (D-30) contain exactly the songs received over BLE in the running gathering, in the order the leader pushed them: the current song and the ones before it. No upcoming song, no title of a song still to come, no placeholder for a text that has not arrived, even when the singer's own library holds that song. The singer's local library is a different thing: it is browsed in browse state (D-40), is never merged into the session page, and a song read there is not "part of the session" until the leader pushes it. When the gathering ends, the received songs are discarded [FR-DATA-01] and only their titles stay in the gathering memory [FR-DATA-03]. *Why:* the leader decides what the fire sings and when; reading ahead undermines that and tempts singers to request what is coming anyway. It also keeps a singer without the library from ever holding more than what was broadcast, which is the spirit of FR-LIB-22. FR-SING-03's optional "upcoming songs if the leader shares them" is therefore not used in phase 1 (Q17).

**To investigate: a magnifier mode (*vergrootglas*).** Besides scrolling a page of text at one size (D-28, D-29), a different way of reading is to be evaluated before the lyric view is built: the lyrics and the songlist are laid out at a smaller, fixed size that shows a whole song or a long stretch of the setlist at once, and the singer zooms in and out with a pinch and moves over the text like a magnifying glass, by dragging. The purpose is readability of both the lyrics and the setlist: an overview of the song at a glance, and large text where the eyes are. When the magnifier mode is on, the lyrics and the setlist are rendered at a size chosen to work with zooming (a sub-optimal size for plain reading, optimal under the glass); when it is off, D-28 applies. The investigation has to answer, with real songs on a real phone in the dark: whether the glass can be moved reliably with cold fingers and one hand (FR-SING-11 asks for simple, reliable gestures); whether it can respect FR-DISP-06, which forbids needing horizontal scrolling to read a line, for example by snapping the glass to whole lines or by zooming line height only; how follow-the-leader (FR-SING-12) and the current-line mark (D-16) behave under the glass; and how the mode interacts with the text-size steps of FR-DISP-05 and the system font scaling of NFR-ACC-02. Outcome options: adopt it as a third way of reading alongside the text-size steps and fit-to-width, limit it to the songlist, or drop it. D-48 weighs against it: zooming and panning is a reason to play with the phone, so the investigation has to show a readability gain the text-size steps cannot give. Open question Q13; no decision yet.

### 6.2 Singer mode: around the lyric view

Section 6.1 is the page the singer reads. This section is everything else a singer meets between joining and leaving: the way in, the states of the connection, requests, the library, and the delegate role.

#### Joining and the connection

**D-35 — Joining from a QR code is one screen that reports what it does.** When the camera app opens the join link, the app skips the welcome and home screens (D-05, D-22) and shows the **joining screen** (*deelnemen*), a configuration screen: the gathering name when the code carries one, the fingerprint words (D-36), and one status line that changes as the app works: *Kampvuur zoeken…* while scanning for the identifier in the code, *Gevonden, controleren…* while the gathering state is verified against the key pinned from the code, then straight into singer mode and the lyric view, with the join code from the QR code supplied without asking [FR-GATH-20]. If nothing signed by that key is in range, the screen keeps looking for 60 seconds with *Ga dichter bij de kampvuurleider staan* and then offers *Opnieuw zoeken* and *Naar start*; it never gives up silently. If a gathering with the identifier is found but its state is not signed by the key in the code, the screen says *Dit is niet het kampvuur van de QR-code* and offers only *Naar start* [FR-GATH-19]. The first join ever, by QR code or from the list, is preceded by the display-name dialog of D-26, which can be skipped. *Why:* the QR code is the trust anchor against a fake gathering; the singer should see the check happen and see it fail, in words a child understands.

**D-36 — The fingerprint is always in the header strip.** The two fingerprint words [FR-GATH-18] stand after the gathering name in the singer header strip (D-20): `Kampvuur 18 okt · appel-vuur · verbonden`. When the strip is too narrow the gathering name is truncated with an ellipsis, never the fingerprint or the state. On the home screen (D-22) every gathering card shows its fingerprint under the name, and when two gatherings in range have the same or a very similar name, the join area shows one line above the cards: *Twee kampvuren met bijna dezelfde naam: vraag de leider naar de twee woorden.* *Why:* the fingerprint only helps if it is visible without looking for it, both when choosing and after joining.

**D-37 — Losing the leader, and the end of a gathering, are banners, never dialogs.** While contact with the leader is lost the lyric page stays as it is and the header strip reads *opnieuw verbinden…* [FR-GATH-10]; nothing covers the text. When the gathering cannot be found for the configured time (default five minutes) [FR-GATH-11], or the leader ends it [FR-GATH-09], the strip reads *beëindigd* and a banner slides in above the round buttons: *Het kampvuur is niet meer te vinden* or *De leider heeft het kampvuur beëindigd*, with *De teksten blijven zichtbaar tot je vertrekt* and one button, *Terug naar start*. The lyrics received over the air are discarded and the gathering memory is written when the singer takes that step or leaves (D-34) [FR-DATA-01, FR-DATA-03]; queued requests are discarded with a line in the banner [FR-REQ-03]. A library download instruction from the leader [FR-LEAD-30] would arrive the same way, as a banner with *Downloaden* and *Niet nu*; by D-66 there is none in phase 1, and this sentence stays only so the shape is known should Q21 bring the instruction back. *Why:* at the fire a modal dialog over the lyrics is the worst possible interruption; a banner informs and waits.

#### Requests

**D-38 — The request tab shows the singer's own requests and their state.** The songlist sheet (D-31) has three tabs: *Lijst* (the setlist), *Verzoeken* (requests) and *Liedboek* (the library, D-40). The *Verzoeken* tab lists the singer's own requests, newest on top, each with a state chip: *in afwachting*, *geaccepteerd (plek 5)* when the position is known, *afgewezen*, *gezongen*, and *wachtrij* while the singer is not connected, with the note that it is sent on reconnection [FR-REQ-03, FR-REQ-05]. A pending or queued request has a cross to withdraw it [FR-REQ-04]. The tab header shows the count against the limit, *2 van 3* [FR-REQ-06], and the button *Ander lied aanvragen* opens the free-text form: one field of at most 100 characters with a counter, *Verzenden*, nothing else [FR-REQ-02]. Requesting a song that is in the local library goes through the *Liedboek* tab (D-40) [FR-REQ-01]. The optional "+1" on other singers' requests [FR-REQ-07] is held back by D-48: the tab shows the singer's own requests only. *Why:* the singer must always know what became of a request; one tab that is also the way to make one keeps the mental model small, and other people's requests are something to watch rather than to sing.

**D-39 — One heart, one star.** A **heart** always talks to the leader: on a song in the setlist page or the *Lijst* tab it is a like (D-32), a request on the leader's side, grouped and counted [FR-LEAD-12], withdrawn by tapping again. A **star** always talks to the singer themselves: it marks a favourite in the library [FR-LIB-32], stored on the device and never sent. By D-48 the star is only offered in the library outside a gathering, where it is a filter; while joined, the *Liedboek* tab shows neither stars nor a favourites filter. The lyric page shows the heart (D-32); neither control appears where its meaning would be ambiguous. *Why:* three controls with the same icon and three meanings is how children end up requesting songs by accident.

#### The library while singing

**D-40 — The library is the third tab of the songlist sheet, and browsing never loses the current song.** The *Liedboek* tab is the whole combined songbook [FR-LIB-30]: a search field at the top that matches titles and fragments of lyrics [FR-LIB-31], a row of filter chips (library, language, tag), and the list. It is reachable at any time while joined [FR-LIB-33] and the sheet expands to full height when the search field has focus, so the keyboard does not hide the results (Q16). Tapping a song opens it on the lyric page in **browse state**: the header strip reads *bladeren* instead of the connection state, the song's text is shown exactly as the setlist page shows songs, and the chip *Terug naar het huidige lied* of D-29 is the one-action return [FR-SING-14]. The toolbar (D-17) gains *Dit lied aanvragen* in browse state, which sends the request [FR-REQ-01] and returns to the current song. A song pushed by the leader while browsing pulls the view back to it with the cue of D-43 [FR-SING-02]. *Why:* singers look things up while others sing; the sheet is already the place for everything that is not the lyrics, and a separate full screen would make the way back a two-step affair.

**D-41 — Variants are switched from the song-info sheet.** When a song belongs to a variant group [FR-LANG-06] and another member is in a local library, the song-info sheet (D-45) lists the variants as *Toon in: English*; choosing one swaps the text for the variant on this singer's device only, keeping the position at the same canonical line where the variants line up and at the top of the song otherwise. The leader is not involved; the entry is absent when no variant is available locally. Nothing on the lyric page itself announces a variant (held back by D-48; v0.5 first drew a chip on the title). *Why:* a Dutch leader can push the Dutch song while an English-speaking singer reads the English words, which is what the variant group exists for; the singer who needs it will look, the others should not be tempted.

#### Display rules and settings

**D-42 — Rotation keeps the line.** When the device is rotated the line at the top of the viewport, or the current line when following the leader, stays in view at the same position in the song [FR-DISP-04]; fit-to-width is re-evaluated for the new width (D-28). The round buttons and the header strip keep their places (D-27, D-30). *Why:* a rotation at the fire is usually accidental; the singer must not lose the line.

**D-43 — A new song is announced, not interrupted.** When the leader pushes a song the page scrolls to its start, its title row carries the orange bar of D-16 for one second, and the device vibrates twice briefly when *Trillen bij nieuw lied* is on in settings (default on) [FR-SING-02]. No sound, no dialog. The same cue plays when the singer is in browse state (D-40). *Why:* the cue must be noticed in the dark and in the noise, and must not need a tap to dismiss.

**D-44 — Display name and vibration live in settings.** Settings has the group *Zingen* with: *Naam voor de leider* (the display name of FR-SING-01: at most 24 characters, hint *Alleen je voornaam of bijnaam*, never prefilled, empty allowed and shown to the leader as *Zanger n*), *Trillen bij nieuw lied* (D-43), *Volumeknoppen scrollen* [FR-SING-11], *Tijd tot "kampvuur niet gevonden"* [FR-GATH-11], *Ontvangst tonen* (D-81, off at every start), and the magnifier switch should Q13 adopt it. A changed display name applies at the next join; while joined the setting says so. *Why:* FR-SING-01 asks the name once; it should still be findable without searching.

**D-81 — The reception bar: on only by choice, off at every start, gone with a tap.** *(Added in v0.14 at the owner's request.)* The settings group *Zingen* (D-44) has the switch *Ontvangst tonen*, which is off whenever the app starts, whatever it was before. When it is on, singer mode shows a floating island at the very bottom of the screen, above the system navigation and inset from the edges, on a night-blue-dark surface (`#0B2545`) in both themes so that all three colours read. The island has one row per document: *Lied* for the current song and *Lijst* for the setlist, each a row of the document's source blocks (`k` of them, ble-protocol 7.3), shrunk to fit the width and at least one pixel wide. A block is **green** when it was received, rebuilt by the erasure code, or fetched over GATT; **white** while it is still to come; **orange** when later blocks of the same document version have arrived and this one has not (missed; the code or a repair will cover it). A complete document is all green; a song from the singer's own library (ble-protocol 10.3 step 3) shows its row all green at once; a new version starts its row white again. Colours change; nothing moves, blinks or counts. The lyric page leaves room under its last line so that the island never covers the line being sung. Tapping anywhere on the island hides it and switches *Ontvangst tonen* off; it comes back only from the settings [FR-SING-05]. *Why:* the owner wants a singer to be able to see how a song and the setlist arrive and where the gaps are, which also helps explain a late song at the fire. *D-48:* a live bar is something to watch, so it stays off unless a singer looks for it in the settings, never survives a restart, and leaves with one tap.

**D-45 — The song-info sheet shows the metadata of the song file, nothing invented.** From the toolbar or a long press on the title (D-33) the info sheet lists, in this order and only when present: title and subtitle; lyricist, composer, melody ("op de wijs van …"), year; copyright with the © sign; licence as its identifier or *licentie van bundel X* when the song carries none; source; tags with their labels; the library's name and the revision installed; the variant group's other members as links (D-41) [FR-SING-15, FR-LIB-20]. For a song received over the air without the library, the sheet says *Ontvangen van de leider* in place of the library line. The sheet is dark in the night theme and closes with a swipe down. *Why:* FR-LIB-20 and FR-LIB-23: show the metadata as it is, never alter it.

#### The delegate role

**D-46 — A singer who becomes a delegate gets one more tab, and nothing of the leader's.** When the leader grants the delegate role (*assistent*, Q15 resolved) [FR-LEAD-40], a banner says *De leider heeft je assistent gemaakt*, the header strip shows *assistent* after the state, and the songlist sheet gains a fourth tab, *Leiden* [FR-LEAD-45]. That tab holds the leader's request list with the same grouping and count (title, requester name, time; accept, push now, decline) [FR-LEAD-41], and the setlist with *Volgende zingen*, *Toevoegen uit liedboek* and, on any song of the *Liedboek* tab, *Nu zingen* [FR-LEAD-42]. Every action goes through the leader's device; when it cannot be reached the action is refused at once with *De leider is niet bereikbaar, probeer het straks opnieuw* and is not retried later [FR-LEAD-43]. The tab has no ending, no join code, no delegates, no download instruction and no hand-over [FR-LEAD-44]. During *opnieuw verbinden…* the tab stays and its actions are disabled; the role survives the reconnection [FR-LEAD-46]. When the role is revoked or the gathering ends, a banner says so and the tab disappears [FR-LEAD-45]. The delegate's own lyric view, likes and requests are unchanged. *Why:* the delegate is a singer with extra buttons, not a second leader; keeping the extras in one tab keeps the lyric view identical for everyone and makes FR-LEAD-44 a matter of what the tab does not contain.

#### After the gathering

**D-49 — The words leave with the fire; the list of songs stays as a memory.** Every song the leader pushed to a singer over BLE is removed from the singer's app when the gathering has ended: when the singer leaves (D-34), when the leader ends it (D-37), or when it is treated as ended after the configured time (D-37) [FR-DATA-01]. Nothing of the texts survives, not in a cache, not in a backup [NFR-PRIV-02]. Songs from a library the singer imported themselves are untouched [FR-DATA-02]. What the singer's app keeps is the **gathering memory** (*herinnering*): the gathering's name, the date, and the setlist as the ordered list of song titles with their song and library identifiers, and nothing about people or devices [FR-DATA-03]. The memories are a configuration screen, *Herinneringen*, reached from the icon row of the home screen (D-21): one card per campfire with name, date and the number of songs; opening one shows the setlist as titles. A title whose song is in a local library opens that song for reading; the others are plain text with a note that the words came from the leader that evening [FR-DATA-05]. The screen has *Deel de lijst* (export the titles as plain text through the share sheet) and *Verwijder*, per memory and for all at once [FR-DATA-04]; memories are never sent anywhere by the app. The writing of the memory happens at the moment the gathering ends for the singer, in the same step that removes the lyrics (D-34, D-37), so the two can never be out of step. *Why:* the lyrics belong to the library and its licence, and a singer without that library held them only as a guest of the evening [FR-LIB-22]; the list of what was sung is the singer's own evening, worth keeping and harmless to keep. The memories screen is deliberately plain, in line with D-48: no photos, no counts of likes, no sharing beyond a text list.

#### Room for phase 2

**Karaoke (P2).** The lyric page already carries the current-line mark (D-16) and the line numbering of the song format's canonical line list; karaoke highlighting [FR-KAR-03] will drive that mark from the gathering clock and add a thin progress line under the current line, with the next lines visible as they are now. The toolbar (D-17) reserves a *Meelopen* toggle for the local switch-off of FR-KAR-09 that appears only in phase 2. Nothing in section 6.1 or 6.2 needs to change for it.

### 6.3 Leader mode (*kampvuurleider*)

The leader prepares at home, with light and time, and leads at the fire, in the dark and with one hand. The preparation screens are configuration screens (section 2); from the moment the gathering starts the leader is on the gathering family's black screen, in the same lyric view as the singers [FR-LEAD-04], with more buttons. D-48 is written for singers; the leader is an adult with a job to do and gets the controls that job needs, but no more.

#### Preparation: libraries

**D-50 — Libraries are loaded from a repository or from a file, and the leader always knows which revision is installed.** The *Bibliotheek* screen (reached through *Bibliotheken* in the *Liedboek*'s toolbar, D-52; amended in v0.16) lists the installed libraries, the default one first [FR-LIB-02], each with its name, the number of songs, the installed version or revision [FR-LIB-17], the date of the release archive, and a colour square (D-51). While the default library is not installed, the screen offers it by name under *Standaardbibliotheek*: on the empty screen as the main button, and at the top of *Bibliotheek toevoegen*; it is added only on that tap, through the same release, size and licence steps as any other [FR-LIB-02, FR-LIB-12] *(amended in v0.15)*. *Bibliotheek toevoegen* offers two routes: **online**, by pasting or scanning the URL of a repository, a release index or a `.zbl` archive (the app fetches the newest release; song-format section 12) [FR-LIB-10, FR-LIB-14]; and **from a file**, by opening a `.zbl` or zip file from the phone's storage, a shared file or an SD card, for phones that never have internet [FR-LIB-16]. Both routes show the library's licence summary and full text before the import completes [FR-LIB-21] and end with the import report: songs imported, skipped (by file name and reason) and warnings [FR-LIB-07]. A library row has *Bijwerken* (update, with the "what changed" list afterwards: added, changed, removed [FR-LIB-11]), *Uit* / *Aan* (disable without removing) and *Verwijderen* [FR-LIB-02]. The previous revision stays in use until an update is complete [FR-LIB-15]. Nothing downloads without a tap, except when *Automatisch controleren op wifi* is on, which only checks and asks [FR-LIB-12, FR-LIB-13]. *Why:* the leader must be able to say which version the fire is singing from, and a library must be addable at a campsite with no internet from a file a friend hands over.

**D-51 — Each library has a colour; the combined songbook shows it as a small square.** When a library is installed it is assigned the next colour from a fixed list of eight distinct, colour-blind-safe colours (orange `#FF8A3D`, sky blue `#4FC3F7`, green `#6CC24A`, yellow `#FFD166`, purple `#B388FF`, pink `#FF7EB6`, teal `#2EC4B6`, grey `#BDBDBD`); the ninth library starts over with a distinguishing dot. The colour is shown in the library list as a legend and as a **12 dp square before the title** of every song in the combined songbook [FR-LIB-30], in the setlist editor and on the leader's page; it is never shown to singers (D-48). The leader can change a library's colour from the library row. *Why:* a scouting group typically has its own library next to a shared one, and the leader wants to see at a glance where a song comes from without a second line of text.

**D-52 — One songbook, sorted and filtered, with a reset that is always visible.** The *Liedboek* screen is the combined list of all enabled libraries [FR-LIB-30]: colour square, title, and in the secondary colour the language code and, when known, the usage (*3× · laatst 12 okt*). When two or more visible rows carry the same title, each of them also shows its library's name in the secondary text (*Advocaatje · Kampvuurliederen · nl*), so the choice never rests on the colour square alone; rows with a unique title stay as short as before. The same rule applies wherever songs are listed with their colour square: the setlist editor (D-54), the setlist block and separators (D-55, D-56) and the leader's songlist sheet (D-58). *(Amended in v0.7.)* The toolbar above the list holds: a **search field** that filters on a word in the title and, with the switch *ook in de tekst*, on a fragment of the lyrics [FR-LIB-31]; the **sort** toggle *A–Z* / *Z–A*, with two more orders in its menu, *vaakst gezongen* and *laatst gezongen*; and **filter chips** in two groups: metadata from the library (library, language, tag [FR-LIB-30]) and metadata from use (*nog nooit gezongen*, *dit jaar gezongen*, *meer dan 5×*). A **reset** button (*Wis filters*) is shown whenever any search, sort or filter differs from the default and clears them all at once. The usage metadata (number of times a song was pushed by this device and the date of the last time) is recorded on the leader's device from its gathering memories, stored locally, never sent [NFR-PRIV-01], and removed with the memories [FR-DATA-07]. *Why:* a leader with 400 songs over three libraries needs to find "that one about the mole" and to avoid singing the same twelve songs every evening; both are answered by the list itself, not by a separate statistics screen.

**D-53 — Reading a song in preparation adds it to the setlist being edited.** Tapping a song opens it in the lyric view (gathering-family styling, day theme by default outside a gathering, the song-info sheet of D-45 available). In preparation the view carries one primary button, *Toevoegen aan "<setlist>"*, naming the setlist currently being edited (D-54); when no setlist is being edited the button reads *Toevoegen aan setlist…* and asks which one, offering to create a new one. A song already in that setlist shows *Staat in de setlist* instead. *Why:* reading the words is how a leader decides; the decision should be one tap away from the text.

**D-54 — Setlists are managed first, filled second.** The *Setlists* screen (reached through *Setlists* in the *Liedboek*'s toolbar, D-52; v0.17, to be confirmed by the owner) lists the prepared setlists with name, number of songs and last use [FR-LEAD-02]. It offers *Nieuwe setlist*, and per row *Naam wijzigen*, *Dupliceren* and *Verwijderen* (with confirmation). Opening a setlist shows its songs in order with their colour squares; songs are reordered by drag, removed by swipe, and added by *Liederen toevoegen*, which opens the songbook (D-52) in **editing mode**: a banner at the top names the setlist and shows the number of songs it holds (*Zomerkamp · 14 liederen*), every row starts with a **checkbox** that adds the song to the setlist or removes it again, each row showing title, language and the library's colour and name, and the reading view's button (D-53) targets the same setlist; *Klaar* in the banner returns to the setlist. *(Amended in v0.7 from the owner's description.)* Prepared setlists are kept across gatherings [FR-DATA-06]; the setlist a gathering used is copied into it at the start (D-55), so changes at the fire never alter the prepared one. *Why:* the user's instruction: add, change or remove setlists before adding or removing songs; separating the two steps keeps the songbook screen free of setlist chrome until the leader asks for it.

**D-59 — Setlists can be made on a computer, imported like a library, and exported.** A setlist is a small plain-text file (`.zbs`, `design/song-format.md` section 13) that a leader can write or edit on a computer as easily as on the phone: a `{title: …}` and `{meta: id …}` line, then one song per line as `<library id>/<song id>`, optionally followed by `#` and the title for readability, or `? <title>` for a song the author knows only by name. The *Setlists* screen (D-54) has *Importeren* (from a file on the phone, a shared file, or a URL, the same two routes as a library, D-50) and, per setlist, *Exporteren*, which hands the file to the share sheet or saves it. A library may also ship setlists: files under `setlists/` in the repository and the release archive are imported together with the library and appear on the *Setlists* screen under the library's name and colour, read-only, with *Dupliceren* to make an editable copy. On import, every line is resolved against the installed libraries; songs that are not installed are kept in the list as *niet gevonden* with their identifiers, so the leader can install the missing library and the list completes itself, and so an exported list survives a round trip. The file format, the `setlists/` directory and the manifest field are specified in `design/song-format.md` section 13 (Q18, resolved). *Why:* the user's instruction: a leader prepares on a phone or a computer, and a group shares its evening programme as a file or with its library. Identifiers rather than titles keep the file unambiguous across libraries.

#### Leading at the fire

**D-55 — Starting copies the setlist and opens the leader's page.** The gathering settings screen (D-25) picks a prepared setlist, or none, and *Starten* copies it into the gathering. The leader then lands on the **leader's page**: the same continuous lyric page as the singers (D-29), with the **setlist block** at the top, the lyrics of every song in setlist order below it, and a separator (D-56) between songs. The setlist block is the ordered list of titles with their colour squares; the song being sung is marked with the orange bar (D-16), songs already sung with a check mark; tapping a title scrolls the page to that song's separator. Unlike the singers' page (D-47), the leader's page holds the whole setlist from the start: the leader needs to see what is coming. The header strip is, in leader mode, the closed leader bar of D-61: `<name> · <fingerprint> · 12 zangers · 1:05` [FR-GATH-12, FR-GATH-18], with a badge for new requests or a warning icon when the platform is about to stop the beacon [FR-GATH-15] or another gathering has come into range [FR-GATH-17]; sliding it open explains and gives the controls. *Why:* FR-LEAD-04 asks for the same lyric view as the singers; putting the setlist on the same page instead of behind a tab means the leader never has to switch views to see what is next.

**D-56 — Every song starts with a separator the leader cannot miss, and the separator has the *Nu* button.** Between songs on the leader's page stands a full-width separator bar, 64 dp high, in night blue (`#13315C`) on the black night theme and in a light blue tint (`#E3F0FF`) in the day theme, with the colour square, the position and title in the title size (*3/12 · Advocaatje*), the state (*gezongen* / *wordt gezongen* / nothing), and at its right the round **Nu** button (56 dp): filled orange on the song the singers have now, black with an orange ring on every other song, a button that can be chosen *(amended in v0.19 at the owner's request)*. *Nu* pushes that song to every singer as the current song [FR-LEAD-01, FR-LEAD-03]: the separator's state becomes *wordt gezongen*, the previous one *gezongen*, the setlist block updates, and the singers' devices switch with their cue (D-43). *Nu* on a song further down skips the ones in between (they stay in the list, unmarked) [FR-LEAD-03]; *Nu* on the song being sung pushes it again, for singers whose transfer failed [FR-LEAD-06]. The separator is what the leader looks for while scrolling, so it is the one bright element allowed on the night screen; it stays far below the brightness of a white panel. *Why:* the user's instruction: a separator at the top of each song that the leader recognises easily, with a button that forces all singers to the same song as they start singing it. One gesture, no dialog, no menu.

**D-57 — The leader's buttons are the singer's three plus the QR code.** The leader's page has the same column of round buttons at the bottom right (D-30): ▲ previous song, ≡ songlist, ▼ next song, navigating the leader's own page without pushing anything. Above them, after a 24 dp gap, sits a fourth round button with the **QR code** icon. It opens the join QR screen at any time while the gathering is open, before the first song and between songs alike [FR-GATH-19]: the code black on a white panel, as large as the screen allows, brightness raised, with the gathering name, the fingerprint and, when a join code is set and not QR-only, the code as text below it [FR-DIST-02, FR-GATH-21]; a tap anywhere closes it and restores the brightness. The ▲ and ▼ buttons move between separators; pushing stays with *Nu*, so a leader cannot change the singers' song by scrolling. *Why:* late arrivals are the rule at a campfire; showing the code must be one tap from wherever the leader is.

**D-58 — The leader's songlist sheet is about songs; the gathering is run from the top bar.** *(Amended with D-61.)* The ≡ button opens the leader's version of the sheet (D-31) with the tabs *Setlist* (the same list as the block on the page, with reorder, remove, and *Toevoegen uit liedboek* [FR-LEAD-03]) and *Liedboek* (the songbook of D-52 with, per song, *Nu zingen* and *Toevoegen*). Requests, singers, delegates, the join code, the download instruction, statistics and ending the gathering are rows in the leader bar's drawer (D-61), so the two views of FR-LEAD-04 are: the page with its sheet for the songs, the drawer for the gathering. The contents of the *Verzoeken* and *Zangers* rows, and ending the gathering, are still to be described (Q14). *Why:* one sheet with tabs is what the singers already have (D-31, D-46); the leader's is the same thing for songs, and the things that are about people and the gathering sit where the owner wants them, at the top.

#### Statistics, opt-in

**D-60 — The leader's device keeps statistics of a campfire only after a deliberate opt-in, nothing in them can point to a person, and they leave the phone only as a file the leader exports by hand: there is no server.** *(Decided 2026-10-19 after the investigation of Q19: export only, no server; field tests with known groups under the test plan for the BLE questions; a local statistics screen for the leader, D-62. The app therefore still sends nothing to the developer, and FR-GEN-08, NFR-PRIV-01, the privacy policy and the Play declaration stand as they are; the assessment records the local records and the export file.)* During a gathering the leader's app can record, per campfire: the date; for each pushed song its identifier and library identifier, the time it was pushed relative to the start of the gathering and the time until the next push (its duration), and whether it was skipped; the number of joined singers over time (counts only); BLE figures from the leader's own radio (advertising set counts, frames repeated or repaired, connection attempts and failures, signal-strength bands); and the app, format and protocol versions. It never records: display names, the gathering name (free text that may name a person), device identifiers or addresses of any phone, anything hashed or pseudonymous that could be matched later, absolute clock times finer than the date, location, or anything about an individual singer; singers' devices record nothing at all. The feature is **off by default** and is switched on only in settings, under *Statistieken*, through a screen that lists exactly the items above, in the plain language of FR-GEN-08, with the switch at the bottom; there is no prompt, banner or nudge anywhere else. While it is off, nothing is recorded. While it is on, the records stay on the leader's device until the leader, from the same settings screen or from the statistics screen (D-62), chooses **Delen**: the app writes a JSON or CSV file and opens the platform's share sheet, so the leader picks mail, WhatsApp, Signal, a file manager or any other installed app and sends it wherever they want, to the author or anyone else. The app itself never transmits the records anywhere and has no server; the share sheet is the only way out. Records are deleted with "remove all data" [FR-DATA-07] and are not part of the gathering memory. *Why:* the owner's instruction, 2026-10-19: collect song durations, start and stop, singer numbers and BLE link quality to improve the app, as an opt-in chosen deliberately in the configuration, anonymised without hashing, and only then exportable or submittable. The protocol's open questions about real-world BLE behaviour (ble-protocol Q2, Q3) are exactly what these figures would answer.

**What the developer wants to know, and how it is met.** Behind the feature stands a legitimate need: the author wants to know how the app performs (BLE reach, repair rates, battery) and how it is used (songs, durations, group sizes). The routes considered in Q19, with the decision: the first three are adopted, the fourth is free, the fifth is available through the closed test the project runs anyway. A server of the author's was considered and dropped: it would add a network peer and a transmission to the developer, which FR-GEN-08, NFR-PRIV-01, the privacy policy and the Play declaration all rule out.

| Route | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Share only, no server** (adopted). The opt-in records stay as above and leave the phone only through the share sheet (mail, WhatsApp, Signal, files, any installed app); leaders who want to help send it by hand. | The same figures from volunteers, with a human in the loop; no network peer, no server, no change to the Play declaration. | Fewer, self-selected data points; the privacy screen still has to mention the local records. |
| **Field tests with known groups.** The protocol's open questions (ble-protocol Q2, Q3) are answered by measured sessions with consenting leaders using a test build that logs locally, under the test plan (FRD section 19). | Exactly the BLE and battery figures that matter, with context; nothing in the released app. | Effort per session; no view of everyday use. |
| **A local statistics screen for the leader.** The same records shown to the leader only (*Dit seizoen: 14 kampvuren, 212 liederen, gemiddeld 23 zangers*), never leaving the phone. | Leaders find it useful; the author learns from the leaders they talk to. | No aggregate for the author. |
| **Platform signals that already exist.** Play Console installs and Android vitals (crashes, ANRs, from users who opted into Google's diagnostics), GitHub release download counts of the library archives, issue reports. | Usage and stability at the population level, with no code in the app. | Coarse; nothing about songs or BLE. |
| **A closed-test cohort.** The Play closed test the project needs anyway (RUNBOOK) with testers who agreed up front to share exports. | Richer data from people who signed up for it. | A test programme to run. |

#### The leader's top bar

**D-61 — A small bar at the top of the leader's page that slides open into a drawer.** In leader mode the header strip of D-55 is the closed state of the **leader bar** (*leiderbalk*): one line, 32 dp, with the gathering name, the fingerprint, the singer count and the time since the start (`Kampvuur 18 okt · appel-vuur · 12 zangers · 1:05`), plus a small handle in the middle and, when something needs attention, a badge at the right: the number of new requests, or a warning icon [FR-GATH-15, FR-GATH-17]. Dragging the handle down, or tapping the bar, slides the drawer open over the top part of the page; the lyrics stay visible below it and keep scrolling. The drawer holds, top to bottom: the **live statistics** of D-62; the **leader functions** as rows: *Verzoeken* with the pending count [FR-LEAD-10] (D-63), *Assistenten* with the delegates and *Assistent toevoegen* [FR-LEAD-40] (D-73; the singer count itself is in the bar and the statistics, there is no singer list), *Deelnamecode* (shows the code; nothing in QR-only mode; not changeable, D-71) [FR-GATH-05, FR-GATH-21], *Statistieken delen* (when opt-in is on, D-60; opens the share sheet), the information row *Ander kampvuur in de buurt* while one is detected (D-68), and last, set apart, *Kampvuur beëindigen* with the confirmation of FR-GATH-08 (D-69); and at the bottom edge a handle to slide it shut. There is no download-instruction row (D-66). A swipe up, a tap on the lyrics, or the start of a song by *Nu* closes it. The drawer is the leader's control view in the sense of FR-LEAD-04 for everything about the gathering; the ≡ sheet (D-58) keeps everything about songs. Singers have no such bar: their strip is one line and does nothing on a drag (D-20, D-48). *Why:* the owner's instruction: a small bar that sits there during the campfire and can be enlarged by sliding it open, as the one place where more leader functions appear over time. It leaves the lyrics alone until the leader pulls, and new functions get a row in the drawer instead of a new button on the page.

**D-62 — Live statistics in the drawer, the season in settings.** The top of the drawer shows the campfire so far, in four large figures and one line: time since the start, songs sung, singers now with the evening's maximum in small print, and link quality as one word (*goed* / *matig* / *zwak*, from the repair and failure rates of the leader's radio) with the last song's duration below. The figures are computed live from the same records as D-60 and shown whether or not the opt-in is on; what the opt-in governs is whether anything is kept after the gathering ends. The season view, *Statistieken* in settings, is the local statistics screen: per campfire the date, songs, maximum singers and duration; totals for the season (*14 kampvuren, 212 liederen, gemiddeld 23 zangers*); the most and least sung songs from the leader's own usage metadata (D-52); the opt-in switch with its explanation; *Delen* (the share sheet: mail, WhatsApp, Signal, files, any installed app); and *Wissen*. It is reachable from settings and from the drawer's statistics block. Nothing in it names a person [NFR-PRIV-01]. *Why:* the leader wants to know, tonight, whether the fire is still reaching everyone and how long the evening has run; and over a season, what was sung. The author learns from files leaders choose to share.

#### Requests and singers

**D-63 — Requests are grouped by song, newest group on top, with three actions and one switch.** The *Verzoeken* row of the drawer (D-61) opens the request list as a full-height sheet. Each row is one requested song: title (or the typed text for a free-text request, in italics), the library colour when the song is known, a **count badge** when more than one singer asked (*×4*) [FR-LEAD-12], the time of the latest request, and, in the secondary colour, the display names of the requesters, folded to *Jip, Noor en 2 anderen* until tapped [FR-LEAD-10]. The row's actions sit at its right as three small round buttons: **✓ Accepteren** (appends the song to the setlist after the current song, the row moves to a greyed *geaccepteerd* section at the bottom), **▶ Nu** (pushes it at once, as D-56, and marks it sung) and **✕ Afwijzen** (removes the row) [FR-LEAD-11]. Every action informs the requesters through their request state [FR-REQ-05]. Liked songs (D-32) are requests like any other and appear here with their count. At the top of the sheet sits one switch, **Geen verzoeken meer vanavond**: when on, singers' request buttons are disabled with that text and the list only shows what is already there [FR-LEAD-13]. The badge on the closed leader bar counts rows not yet handled. *Why:* the owner asked for a fitting suggestion; grouping is what makes twenty children's taps into a short list, and three buttons on the row is faster in the dark than a menu.

**D-64 — Singer list.** *Superseded the same day by D-73 (no singer list; delegates by helper code).* The v0.7 text proposed a list of joined singers with a delegate checkbox per row; the owner dropped it because singers are anonymous and only their number matters. The owner's description given for this item (song rows with language and library colour, a checkbox at the start of the line, a counter at the top) fit the setlist editor and is applied there (D-54).

#### The song bar's other buttons

**D-65 — Each song bar also carries *Wis* and the language button.** The separator bar of D-56 has, left of *Nu*, two smaller text buttons. **Wis** is enabled only on the song being sung and clears it: nothing is being sung, singers keep the text on screen with the state *gewist* in their strip, and the bar's state returns to blank [FR-LEAD-06]. The **language button** shows the language code of the variant (*EN*) and is enabled only when the library holds another language variant of that song [FR-LANG-06]; pressing it swaps the bar's song for the variant, and *Nu* then pushes the variant [FR-LEAD-07]. Both are hidden, not greyed, when they can never apply to that song, so the bar stays as clean as the owner described it. *Why:* the owner's instruction: clear and language as buttons in the song bar, the language button only when another language is available.

#### No download instruction

**D-66 — Singers receive what the leader pushes; there is no "download this library" instruction in phase 1.** When singers join, the leader's app pushes the current song and, from then on, every song the leader starts; a singer whose copy did not arrive completely gets it again through the protocol's repair path, or asks for it with the request mechanism of D-38. The leader therefore has no *Bibliotheek doorsturen* row (removed from D-61), the singer has no download banner (removed from D-37), and the revision view of FR-LEAD-31 is not shown (D-64). *Why:* the owner's decision, 2026-10-19: no download instruction; the setlist and the pushed songs go over BLE, and the singer app asks when something is missing. FRD v0.9 (2026-10-19) made this final: FR-LEAD-30 and FR-LEAD-31 are withdrawn and FR-LEAD-32 now simply says that lyrics always go over BLE to singers that lack them; the BLE protocol keeps its `download` document reserved for a later phase (Q21, resolved).

#### Resuming, warnings and the end

**D-67 — After a crash the welcome screen is replaced by two buttons.** When the app starts within the resume window of FR-GATH-13 after it died while leading, the welcome screen (D-04) is not shown; in its place, on the same background, stand the gathering's name and fingerprint, the time since it died, and two buttons: **Hervatten** (resume the same gathering with the same identifier and keys, singers reconnect by themselves) and **Opnieuw beginnen** (discard the resume data and go to the home screen). When the window has expired the resume data is deleted and the normal welcome screen appears [FR-DATA-08]. The window length is a setting under *Leiden* (default as the FRD says, configurable). *Why:* the owner's instruction: at a crash the startup screen is replaced by resume or restart buttons.

**D-68 — Battery is settled before the fire; other gatherings are a quiet indicator.** The gathering settings screen (D-25) runs a check before *Starten*: when battery saver is on, or the battery is below 30 %, it shows a line *Zet de batterijbespaarstand uit en gebruik een powerbank: een kampvuur duurt uren* with a button to the system setting; starting is still allowed [FR-GATH-15]. If the platform's state changes during the gathering, only the warning icon of the leader bar (D-61) appears; nothing interrupts. Other gatherings coming into range [FR-GATH-17] never produce a notification: the system is resilient against them, and the leader is not actively informed. The leader bar's drawer shows a row *Ander kampvuur in de buurt: "<name>"* while one is detected, as information only. *Why:* the owner's instruction: mention the battery before the start and suggest a power bank; no active warnings about other gatherings, an indicator in the bar at most.

**D-69 — The setlist ends with a closing bar.** Below the last song on the leader's page, as the last item of the setlist block and the page, stands the **closing bar** (*afsluitbalk*), in the same style as the song separators (D-56) but with the text *Einde van de setlist* and one button, **Afsluiten**. Pressing it asks the confirmation FR-GATH-08 requires (*Het kampvuur voor iedereen beëindigen?*); on *Ja* the beacon stops, singers see the ended state (D-37), and the leader sees the **summary screen**, a configuration screen: name, date, time sung, songs sung, the evening's maximum number of singers, and *Terug naar start*. That summary is what the leader's gathering memory keeps [FR-DATA-03], with the participant count; requests, the gathering's setlist copy and connection data are discarded [FR-DATA-06], the resume data too [FR-DATA-08]. The *Kampvuur beëindigen* row in the drawer (D-61) does the same thing for an evening that ends before the list does. *Why:* the owner's instruction: at the end of the song list the closing bar is presented as part of the setlist with the close button.

**D-70 — Follow-the-leader follows the song, not the line, in phase 1.** The leader has the same presentation of the songs as the singers; the only position the leader sends is the start of a song, through *Nu* (D-56). Singers with follow-the-leader on (FR-SING-12) are taken to the start of each pushed song and scroll by themselves from there. The leader does not mark the current line or verse; the SHOULD of FR-LEAD-05 is not met in phase 1 (Q20). *Why:* the owner's description of the leader's view has no line marker, and a leader tapping lines while singing is the kind of phone attention D-48 wants to avoid, on the leader's side too.

#### Fixed for the evening

**D-71 — The join code and the QR-only setting are fixed for one gathering.** They are chosen on the gathering settings screen (D-25) and cannot be changed while the gathering runs; the join QR code is valid for exactly that gathering. The *Deelnamecode* row of the drawer (D-61) only shows the code as text, and shows nothing in QR-only mode, where the QR screen (D-57) is the only way in [FR-GATH-05, FR-GATH-21]. A leader who needs a different code or wants to switch to QR-only ends the gathering (D-69) and starts a new one, which takes under a minute and gives singers a fresh code to scan. *Why:* the owner's decision, 2026-10-19: one QR code runs for one gathering; a change means stop and restart. One key, one code, one evening keeps the trust story of FR-GATH-19 simple, and FR-LEAD-44's "changing the join code" is thereby reserved to nobody at all.

**D-72 — Hand-over of the leader role is not in phase 1; stop and restart is the way.** FR-GATH-14 (MAY) is not implemented; FRD open question Q5 is closed accordingly. When a leader has to stop, for an empty battery or any other reason, they end the gathering (D-69) and another adult starts a new one from the same prepared setlist, which the first leader can hand over as an exported file (D-59) or which the group already has in its library. Singers rejoin from the list or by scanning the new QR code. Delegates (D-73) cover the case of a second adult helping during the evening, and the battery check (D-68) the most common cause. *Why:* the owner's decision: option (a) with (b) as the workaround. A real hand-over needs a new signing key, a re-pin on every singer's phone and a transfer of the gathering state, which is the most protocol-heavy item on the list, for a case that a restart handles in a minute.

#### Delegates without a singer list

**D-73 — There is no singer list; the leader sees a count, and delegates are appointed with a helper code.** *(Supersedes D-64.)* The leader's app never lists the joined singers: singers are anonymous to the leader by design, so there is nothing a list could be used for except comparing phones. The drawer (D-61) shows *12 zangers* as a figure, which is also what the statistics keep (D-60, D-62) [FR-GATH-12]. Delegates (*assistenten*) are appointed with a **helper code** (*assistentcode*): the drawer row *Assistent toevoegen* shows a four-digit code, valid for two minutes, large enough to read across the fire; the helper opens their songlist sheet, taps *Assistent worden* on the *Verzoeken* tab and types the code; the leader's device confirms and the helper's app gains the *Leiden* tab (D-46) [FR-LEAD-40, FR-LEAD-45]. Only someone standing with the leader can do this, which is the point. The drawer then lists the delegates, and only them, by display name with an *Intrekken* button each, so the leader always sees who the helpers are [FR-LEAD-45]; the role ends with the gathering or when the helper leaves. FR-GATH-12's SHOULD, a list of singers by display name, is deliberately not met. Requests still show the requester's display name in the request sheet (D-63), as FR-LEAD-10 requires; that is the one place a name appears. *Why:* the owner's decision, 2026-10-19: drop the singer list; only the number matters, and because singers are anonymous there is nothing to do with who is who. FR-LEAD-40 was amended in FRD v0.9 to this mechanism; BLE protocol v0.11 carries it as Control op 9 (Q22, resolved).

**D-64 — Superseded by D-73.** The singer list described in v0.7 is not built.

#### Songs the library does not have

**D-74 — An unresolved setlist entry is skipped at the fire, but never silently.** A setlist entry that does not resolve to an installed song (song-format section 13.5: the song id is unknown in the named library, the named library is not installed, or a title-only entry has no single match) is kept everywhere the setlist is shown and marked with its reason:

- **On the leader's page** (D-55, D-56) the entry has its separator bar like any song, drawn greyed, with the title or identifier and the word *niet gevonden*; directly under the bar, where the lyrics would start, one line in the secondary colour gives the reason: *Dit lied staat niet in een geladen bibliotheek (andere-bundel/lied)*, *Lied "x" bestaat niet in bibliotheek "y"*, or *Geen lied met de titel "Vader Jacob" in de geladen bibliotheken*, followed by *Dit lied wordt overgeslagen*. The bar has no *Nu*, *Wis* or language button; ▲ and ▼ step over it; it is never pushed and singers never see it.
- **In the setlist block** at the top of the page (D-55) the entry is a greyed row with a small warning mark, and the block's header counts them: *12 liederen, 2 worden overgeslagen*.
- **At the start** (D-25, D-55), when the chosen setlist has unresolved entries, the gathering settings screen says so above *Starten* with the same count, so the leader learns it before the fire and not during it. Starting is allowed.
- **In the setlist editor** (D-54) the entry is a greyed row with the reason under it and the actions *Verwijderen* and, for a title-only entry with several candidates, *Kies lied*. The import report of D-59 lists them by line.

Resolution happens by itself when the missing library is installed (D-59). *Why:* the owner's instruction: when an entry is skipped at the fire, show the reason in the songlist under the song's separator bar and do not let it happen silently. A leader who sees a gap in the programme must know why, and the fix (install the library, correct the file) must be obvious from the message.

## 7. Typography and sizes

**D-18 — System font, five lyric sizes, dp targets.** The app uses the platform's default UI font (Roboto on Android, San Francisco on iOS); no font files are shipped. Lyric text sizes are five steps, **18, 22, 28, 36 and 48 sp**, remembered per device, with 28 sp as the default [FR-DISP-05]; the sp unit lets the platform's font scaling apply on top [NFR-ACC-02]. Lines wrap at the view width [FR-DISP-06]. Configuration screens use the platform's standard type scale: display 34 sp for the app name on the welcome screen, title 22 sp, body 16 sp, caption 12 sp. Every tappable control is at least **48 × 48 dp** [NFR-ACC-01], and on gathering screens the main controls (theme, text size, next song) are 56 dp, since they are operated in the dark.

**System bars.** Configuration screens are drawn edge to edge: the status bar is transparent over the image, the navigation bar is transparent over the scrim, and the content keeps the system insets. Gathering screens colour both bars in the theme's background colour; the status bar is hidden in the lyric view while a gathering runs and the keep-screen-on setting is active, so the full height goes to the lyrics.

**Version display.** The welcome screen shows `versionName`. The About screen [FR-GEN-07] shows `versionName (versionCode)`, the supported song-format, archive and BLE protocol versions [NFR-MAINT-01], and the version and revision of each installed library with the date of its release archive. It also shows the short fingerprint of the app signing certificate (`57:86:7B:…:9B:10`) under the heading *Ondertekening* / *Signature*. With it, an adult can check that a copy shared from phone to phone is the genuine app, because the README states the same fingerprint (`design/security-privacy-assessment.md` SP-20, `design/architecture.md` A-18).

## 8. Languages

The app speaks Dutch and English. This section fixes how its own texts are translated and kept in step; the texts themselves live in the locale files, not here. Decided by the owner on 2026-10-20.

**D-75 — Two language layers that never mix.** The **app language** is the language of the app's own texts: buttons, labels, messages, the privacy screen. The project translates it. The **song language** is the language of a song's words, set per song file by its `{meta: language …}` tag [FR-LANG-05]; the app never translates a song, and a Dutch app shows an English song unchanged and the reverse [FR-LANG-04]. A song's translation is a separate song file in the same variant group, switched to from the song-info sheet (D-41) or pushed by the leader with the song bar's language button (D-65) [FR-LANG-06]. Nothing language-dependent travels over Bluetooth: only identifiers, hashes and codes, and every phone renders its own labels, so a Dutch leader and an English singer each read their own app language at the same fire. Free text, such as the gathering name or a typed request, arrives exactly as typed. *Why:* the lyrics belong to the libraries and their licences; the app's job is to frame them in the reader's language.

**D-76 — Dutch is the primary language; English is the fallback.** Every string is written and reviewed in Dutch first; the Dutch UI terms in this document are its source text [FR-LANG-01]. English is complete as well and is the **fallback**: a phone set to a language the app does not support shows English. On Android this means `values/strings.xml` holds English and `values-nl/strings.xml` Dutch; a phone set to Flemish (`nl-BE`) resolves to Dutch by the platform's own rules. The app follows the phone's language by default [FR-LANG-03]; settings has *Taal* with *Automatisch*, *Nederlands* and *English*, and the platform's per-app language picker (Android 13 and later, through `locales_config.xml`) offers the same list. All texts sit in resource files, so a language is one file and no code change [FR-LANG-02]. iOS later uses the same string keys; whether one source is exported to both platforms is for the architecture document (NFR-PORT-01). *Why:* the owner's decision. Dutch is the language of the groups the app is made for; English is the language a German, French or Polish phone in a Dutch camp is most likely to understand.

**D-77 — Generated labels follow the app language; library names are shown as written.** The section labels the app generates when a song file has none (*Couplet 1* / *Verse 1*, *Refrein* / *Chorus*, *Brug* / *Bridge*) follow the app language, also over a song in another language: an English app shows *Chorus* above a Dutch chorus. A label the song file writes itself is shown as written (song-format section 7.1; its Q5 resolved). A library's `name` and `description` are shown as written in its `zangbundel.json`, in the maintainer's language, and are not translatable (song-format Q8 resolved). Tag labels remain translatable per language through the `tags` map; a tag without a label in the app language shows its English label, and without that the tag itself. *Why:* the owner's decisions: labels are part of the app, the library's own description is the maintainer's words.

**D-78 — Rules for every string, enforced by the build.**

- A sentence is never built by joining pieces; it is one string with numbered placeholders (`%1$s`), so a translation can reorder them.
- Counts use the platform's plural resources (*1 zanger*, *12 zangers*), never a hand-made "zanger(s)".
- Dates, times and numbers go through the locale's formatters; lists sort with the locale's collation, dropping a leading article of the song's own language (song-format 6.1) [FR-LANG-07].
- No text in images (D-09); icons carry no letters.
- Layouts use start and end, never left and right, so a right-to-left language later needs no layout work.
- Controls are sized for the Dutch text, which often runs longer than the English; where space runs out, the truncation rules of D-36 apply.
- A string added or changed in one language is added or changed in every supported language in the same change. The release build fails on a missing translation (Android lint's missing-translation check set to error).
- Before each release the app is run once in the platform's pseudo-locales (long text, and mirrored layout) to catch cut-off and hard-coded text.

*Why:* each rule removes a class of translation bug that is cheap to prevent and expensive to find in the field.

**D-79 — One translation per fixed term.** These terms are used everywhere they apply, in the app, the store listing and the privacy texts; the spec term is what the design documents call the thing.

**D-80 — Adding a language, and for now no other languages.** A new app language is: the strings file translated in full, the language added to `locales_config.xml`, the privacy texts (the in-app privacy screen of FR-GEN-08 is part of the strings; `docs/privacy-policy.md` gets the language too), the store listing (`docs/store-listing/<locale>.md`), and the join page (`docs/j/index.html`). Decided 2026-10-20: no languages beyond Dutch and English for now, and no right-to-left support (FRD Q8 closed); the start-and-end rule of D-78 keeps the door open at no cost. *Why:* the owner's decision; one file per language is what FR-LANG-02 promises, and the texts outside the app are where a new language is most easily forgotten.
