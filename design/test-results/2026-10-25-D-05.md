# D-05 over the air, first two-phone run – 2026-10-25

The over-the-air part of D-05 ("push songs, one not held by the singer; the third over the air"), run with the debug screen *Two-phone test* (assessment SP-24) before the app has screens, GATT or repair. It exercises the real beacon codec, carousel, frame codec and reassembler on two phones. **Result: fail before the erasure code** (no pushed document arrived complete by broadcast; the receiving phone heard only a small part of what was sent); **with the code (run 11) every document arrived, in about 38 s**, still far from the 5 s target.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Scenario | D-05, broadcast part only (`design/test-plan.md` section 4) |
| Build | `0.1.0`, versionCode `2026102201`, build type `debug` |
| Participants | 1, the owner, on the owner's own phones |
| Conditions | Indoors, both phones side by side on the desk, on USB to the development PC, screens on; both on Wi-Fi at 5 660 MHz; RSSI −42 to −45 dBm |
| Leader | Samsung Galaxy A52, Android 14: beacon plus 3 legacy data sets plus the extended set, 100 ms interval, TX power high |
| Singer | Huawei Mate 20 Lite, Android 10: legacy carrier only (no extended scanning), `SCAN_MODE_LOW_LATENCY`, `CALLBACK_TYPE_ALL_MATCHES`, filters on the service UUID and company `0xFFFF` |
| Test document | 2 000 random bytes as document 3 (125 legacy frames), never lyrics; new version every 15 s (run 1) and every 60 s (run 2) |

## Measurements

| Measurement | Run 1 (push every 15 s, 2.5 min) | Run 2 (push every 60 s, 6.5 min) |
|---|---|---|
| Pushes sent by the A52 | 12 | 7 |
| New `seq` seen in the Huawei's beacon after a push | every push while listening (10), 0.05 to 0.9 s after it | every push while listening (6), 0.07 to 1.1 s after it |
| Documents complete by broadcast | **0** | **0** |
| Frames received by the Huawei | 5 to 8 per second, of about 30 legacy frames per second sent | 4 per second |
| Beacons received by the Huawei | about 2 per second, of 10 sent | 1.7 per second |

The beacon delays compare log times of two phones whose clocks are set by the network and may differ by a fraction of a second.

Run 3, roles reversed for the beacon only: the Huawei advertised its beacon alone (its single advertising instance; degraded mode, ble-protocol 7.8) at 100 ms, and the A52 listened for 66 s: **3.8 beacons per second of 10 sent**, RSSI −45 dBm.

## Against the criteria

D-05 requires all three songs to arrive, the third over the air. **Fail** for the over-the-air part: at 13 to 17 % reception the 125 frames of a 2 kB document are not all collected before the next push, even with 60 s between pushes. The protocol's answer to missing frames, repair over GATT (7.6, 10.4), is not built yet, so this run cannot say how D-05 fares with it.

## Findings for the design documents

1. **Reception, not transmission, limits the legacy carrier on these phones.** Both scanners report a minority of the advertisements sent to them: the A52 38 % of a lone 10 Hz beacon, the Huawei 13–17 % of the A52's sets. The figures of ble-protocol 7.7 and 12.1 (and the simulation, 4.7 s for a 2 kB song on three sets) assume a singer receives nearly every frame. If such rates hold in the field, routine delivery to a singer without the song depends on repair over GATT, which is exactly the connection load the broadcast-first design set out to avoid (section 4). This bears on ble-protocol Q2 and Q3 and on NFR-PERF-01.
2. **The beacon works as the push signal.** A new `seq` reached the singer within about a second every time, so a singer *with* the song locally (FR-BLE-12) would have switched within the 2 s target.

## Experiments on the cause (runs 4 to 6, same day)

| Run | Change | Huawei received from the A52 | A52 received from the Huawei's lone beacon |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Scan without hardware filters; the app filters on company `0xFFFF` and the service UUID | 5 frames/s, 1.8 beacons/s, no document complete | 3.6 beacons/s (3.8 with filters) |
| 5 | As 4, and the legacy data sets non-scannable (no scan requests) | 5 frames/s, 1.7 beacons/s, no document complete | – |
| 6 | As 5, with a single legacy data set and no extended set | **1 frame/s**, 1.7 beacons/s | – |

What they show:

- **Not the controller's scan filters**: the rates are the same with and without them, in both directions.
- **Not the scan requests on scannable data sets**: non-scannable sets arrive no more often.
- **Not a cap on the total**: one set gave 1 frame/s, four sets about 5, so the Huawei hears each advertiser about one time in six to ten (10 to 17 %), and the A52 a lone advertiser about one time in three (36 to 38 %).

## A third receiver (runs 7 and 8)

The development PC (Intel Wireless Bluetooth, Windows 11) counted advertisements under company `0xFFFF` with the Python package bleak 3.0.2 in active scanning, per sending address, in the same 60-second windows as the phones. The raw Windows event type could not be read, so bleak's merged data was counted; merging can only add events, so these counts are upper bounds.

| Run | Sender | PC heard | Phone heard, same window |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | A52: beacon, 3 legacy sets, extended set, each at 100 ms | beacon 2.3/s; legacy sets 2.3, 2.2 and 2.4/s; extended 0.4/s | Huawei: 1.6 beacons/s, 5 frames/s |
| 8 | Huawei: beacon alone at 100 ms | 2.5/s | A52: 3.8/s |

**Conclusion.** The PC hears one of the A52's five sets about as often as the Huawei's single set (2.2–2.4 against 2.5 per second), so running several sets does not starve each of them, and neither sender shows a shortfall the receivers do not also show. The receivers differ: per advertiser at 100 ms the A52 hears 36–38 %, the PC 23–25 %, the Huawei 10–17 %. Every scanner here reports only a fraction of the advertising events; on the singers that matters, it is a quarter to a sixth. Two senders and three receivers cannot prove the senders keep exactly 100 ms: the A52 hearing 3.8 per second sets a lower bound of 3.8 events per second for the Huawei.

## Wi-Fi off on both phones (runs 9 and 10)

The owner switched Wi-Fi off on the A52 and the Huawei (checked: `wifi_on` 0, "Wi-Fi is disabled"; background Wi-Fi scanning for location stayed enabled). The PC kept its Wi-Fi. Same build and settings as run 7: three legacy sets and the extended set, scannable data sets, hardware scan filters.

| Measurement | Wi-Fi on (runs 1–7) | Wi-Fi off (run 9) |
|---|---|---|
| Huawei: beacons from the A52 | 1.6–2.0/s (16–20 %) | 4.0–4.3/s (40–43 %) |
| Huawei: legacy frames from the A52 | 4–5/s of 30 (13–17 %) | 11–12/s of 30 (37–40 %) |
| Huawei: 2 kB documents complete by broadcast | 0 | 2 of 4 pushes, in **49 s and 44 s** from the new `seq` |
| PC (its Wi-Fi on): each A52 set | 2.2–2.4/s | 1.9–2.6/s |

**Wi-Fi more than doubles what the Huawei hears**, although both phones were on 5 GHz. Even then a 2 kB document takes about 45 s on the legacy carrier, against the 5 s of NFR-PERF-01: at 40 % a frame sent three times in the burst is still missed with a probability of about 0.2, and the 125 frames complete only after several more passes of the normal loop (6.3 s each).

Run 10, the reverse direction with Wi-Fi off, could not run: the Huawei refused its beacon set with status 2 ("too many advertisers") twice, where it had started it in runs 3 and 8 with Wi-Fi on. The system dump names no other advertiser; that a Huawei service takes the single instance when Wi-Fi is off is a guess, not a finding. The test screen crashed on the refusal; it now reports it instead.

Untested: the extended carrier received by a phone (the Huawei cannot, and only the A52 here can lead it); other phones; the field. Both switches used here stay in `AndroidTransport` as diagnostics (`hardwareFilters`, `scannableDataSets`); the test screen runs with the protocol's settings again.

## With the erasure code (run 11)

After the owner approved the erasure coding (`design/ble-protocol.md` v0.21), the same test ran with the coded carousel: A52 leading with three legacy sets and the extended set, Huawei listening on the legacy carrier, **Wi-Fi on again on both phones**, a push every 60 s, five minutes.

| Measurement | Before the code, Wi-Fi on (runs 1–2) | With the code, Wi-Fi on (run 11) |
|---|---|---|
| Frames received by the Huawei | 4–8/s | 4/s (about 13 % of 30) |
| Beacons received | 1.6–2.0/s | 1.6/s |
| 2 kB documents complete by broadcast | 0 of the 16 pushes seen | **4 of 4**, in 37.7, 38.9, 38.3 and 39.7 s |

The code works on the phones: at the same poor reception every document now arrives, in a little under 40 s, where none did before. The simulation predicted 31 s at 17 % (`design/proposals/2026-10-25-erasure-coding.md`); the Huawei heard slightly less. It is still far from 5 s on the legacy carrier, which is FRD Q14. The extended carrier, simulated at 2.7 s for 40 %, still waits for a second phone that can receive it.

## Open questions

- **ble-protocol Q2, Q3:** informed, not answered. Two phones on a desk are not the field.
- **For the owner, if these rates hold:** a singer receives a quarter to a sixth of the legacy frames with Wi-Fi on and about 40 % with it off, so a frame sent in three burst passes is still missed with a probability of 0.2 to 0.6, and the legacy carrier alone cannot deliver a song to a singer without it within 5 s. Options, none decided: rely on the extended carrier where phones receive it (9 frames for 2 kB instead of 125); add forward error correction across frames so any sufficiently large subset completes the document; more passes; singers with the library (the beacon alone suffices); repair over GATT, at the connection cost the broadcast-first design meant to avoid.
