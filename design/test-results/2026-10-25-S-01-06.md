# S-01 to S-06, protocol simulation – 2026-10-25

The simulation scenarios of `design/test-plan.md` section 3.3, run by `SimulationScenariosTest` in `core/protocol`: a `LeaderSession` and 24 to 100 `SingerEngine`s in `SimulatedAir`, in virtual time, with the protocol as of `design/ble-protocol.md` v0.23. **Result with v0.23: S-01, S-04 (table limit), S-05 and S-06 pass; S-02 and S-03 fail their time criteria; S-04 meets the 5 s aim only with the extended carrier. With v0.24, after the owner decided Q24 to Q26: S-03 passes, S-02 comes close (71 of 75 within 5 s), S-04 meets the aim for about half of 50 and 100 singers on the legacy carrier and for all of them with the extended set.**

## What the failures show

1. **The state record waits behind the song (S-03, and part of S-02).** 7.4 sends, within a burst pass, the source frames of every changed document before any repair frame. A singer that misses one or two of the state's 15 source frames must wait for the state's repair frames, which come after the song's 250 source frames, about 8 s later; the repair rule of 7.6 fires first, 3 s after the push. In S-03, 66 of the 78 repairs were for the state. Sending the state record's repair frames directly after its source frames would cost about 1 s of carousel time and spare most of these connections.
2. **A 4 kB song gets almost no erasure code on the legacy carrier (S-02, S-04).** With `n ≤ 256`, a document of 250 source frames gets 6 repair frames; at 10 % loss a singer misses about 25 frames a pass and must wait for the next pass. The code helps documents up to about 128 source frames (2 kB) and very little above.
3. **The repair rule waits too long for big documents (S-02, S-04).** 7.6 repairs when `k` is not expected "within 10 s" counted from the moment of the estimate. A singer receiving the song at 27 frames a second expects it in about 9 s at every moment, so it never repairs, and the song takes 12 to 23 s. Counting the limit from the push (or the group's first frame) would send these singers to GATT at about 2 s; whether the leader's slots carry that for 25 to 100 singers is what the simulation would then have to show.
4. **The extended carrier meets the aim at every group size tried, with no repairs.** On the legacy carrier alone, more data sets made things slower in S-04 (1 set: 7.4 s; 3 sets: 17.4 s): with one set the singers repair sooner and GATT carries the song; with three, the broadcast looks fast enough to the repair rule and they wait. This follows from finding 3.

Degraded mode (S-05) delivered faster than the legacy carrier, by GATT alone, for 25 singers. That rests on the calibrated connection time and on the leader's slots; the field will tell whether 600 ms per connection holds when many phones connect at once.

## After v0.24 (the owner's decisions on Q24 to Q26)

Same code and settings; the burst now opens with the whole state record (Q24) and 7.6 repairs when `k` is not expected within 5 s of the group's start (Q26). Q25 changed nothing. Repeated runs differ by a few tenths of a second, because keys and tokens are random.

What changed with it:

- **The legacy carrier now leans on GATT for songs.** With the whole state first, a 2 kB song takes about 5.7 s by broadcast on three sets without loss, past the 5 s deadline, so even a singer that hears everything fetches the song over GATT at about 2 s and has it at about 3.5 s (`SingerEngineTest`). Broadcast first still holds for the beacon, the state and Info; for songs on the legacy carrier, GATT now carries most of them.
- **The leader's slots are the limit at 50 and 100 singers.** Connections queue (37 refused and retried with 50 singers), and the slowest singers take 8 to 11 s. The extended carrier needs no connection at all and keeps every singer within 3 s.
- The connection timing (600 ms each) is calibrated to two phones; with many phones connecting at once the field may be slower.
