## Against the criteria

F-01 is a survey and has no pass criterion. What it measures, per the test plan:

- **Android major version:** recorded per phone.
- **Multiple advertising support:** recorded; phone B has none.
- **Number of advertising sets that open:** 16 and 1.
- **Extended advertising and its maximum data length:** phone A reports 1650; it accepts at least 1100 bytes in total, but no single structure above 251 bytes.

## Findings for the design documents

1. **The protocol's extended frame cannot be sent as specified.** `design/ble-protocol.md` section 7.2 defines an extended frame of up to 1000 bytes as one manufacturer-data field. A single advertising data structure holds at most 255 bytes by the Bluetooth specification, Android's `AdvertiseData` accepts only one manufacturer-data entry per company identifier, and phone A refuses any single structure above 251 bytes. Phone A does accept 1100 bytes spread over a manufacturer-data structure plus service-data structures. So ~1 kB frames are possible only if the protocol splits a frame over several structures (for example service data under the app's own UUID base, as the probe did), or the extended frame shrinks to about 247 bytes. This is a protocol decision for the owner; it touches sections 7.1 and 7.2, the extended-carrier figures in section 12, and the Android notes in 15.1.
2. **A 2018 mid-range Huawei leads only in degraded mode.** Phone B has a single advertising instance (`isMultipleAdvertisementSupported` false, one set opens), which is exactly the case of ble-protocol 7.8: no broadcast data channel, delivery over GATT, adequate for small groups only. Whether this is common among scout phones is what the real F-01 survey must show; it bears on the "small groups" wording of 7.8 and on FRD open question Q1 about group size. FRD constraint 1 covers phones without peripheral mode; this one has peripheral mode but a single instance, so it can lead, with limited capacity.
3. **Advertising-data updates are fast on both phones** (means of 6 to 17 ms, all successful), far below the 100 ms advertising interval. Updating data is not the bottleneck of the carousel on these phones. Phone A's update time grew with the number of open sets (6.2 ms with 10, 17.1 ms with 16).
4. **Sixteen legacy sets on phone A** is well above the protocol's use of one beacon plus at most three data sets (section 7.1, 10.1 step 4), so the legacy carrier has headroom on such phones.

## Limits of this run

- The probe stops counting at 20 legacy sets and at 1100 bytes of total extended data; phone A reached neither limit on sets but did reach the extended ceiling.
- The multi-structure test rounds the last structure up to at least 19 bytes, so totals are exact to within 18 bytes.
- Indoors at a desk; nothing here says anything about range, loss or battery (F-02, F-04, F-05).
- Raw logs were not kept; the figures above were read from the app's results screen and the debug log line it writes.
